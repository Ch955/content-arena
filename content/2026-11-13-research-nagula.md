## 3. Folk story to retell in our own words: "The farmer's plough and the mother snake"

There are several versions and no single fixed text. Hindu-blog says there are "several versions of Nagula Chavithi story".
**The core that the sources share:** a farmer ploughing his field accidentally destroys a snake mound and kills the baby snakes inside.
In grief, the mother snake bites the farmer's family. The farmer's daughter is found praying to Naga Devata,
asking forgiveness for the harm. The snake is moved and forgives, and the family is brought back to life. In one version the snake gives
the girl nectar to sprinkle on them. From then on the family worships at the snake mound on this day.
- Hindu-blog: https://www.hindu-blog.com/2009/10/nagula-chavithi-story-naga-chaturthi.html
- AstroVed (the daughter praying to a clay snake image, the snake moved): https://www.astroved.com/articles/the-significance-of-naga-panchami
- Bhaktinidhi (parents brought back to life by Naga Devata's grace): https://bhaktinidhi.com/en/nagula-chavithi-katha-in-english
- Boldsky (three baby snakes, the nectar): https://www.boldsky.com/yoga-spirituality/festivals/2017/legends-associated-with-naga-panchami-115969.html
- **Family-friendly retelling note:** don't show death. Say "పాము కోపంతో కుటుంబాన్ని కాటేసింది… కానీ కూతురి క్షమాపణ ప్రార్థన విని మనసు కరిగింది,
  అందరూ మళ్ళీ లేచారు". Moral: **we're careful with nature, and when we do wrong, a sincere sorry matters.**
  The caption should say "retold and adapted from a traditional Nagula Chavithi folk tale". **UNVERIFIED** details: the number of
  babies and the nectar differ between versions, so leave them out. Telugu lines are for the owner to review.
- Another origin story (Shiva and the Halahala poison) is mentioned in search results. I'd skip it because it is mythology-heavy.

## 4. A gentle "Did you know?"

- **Snakes don't really drink milk. Their food is whole prey such as rats, frogs and eggs, and reptiles can't digest milk.**
  Wildlife SOS (wildlife rescue NGO): https://news.wildlifesos.org/debunking-common-myths-surrounding-snakes/ ;
  experts quoted in Deccan Chronicle: https://www.deccanchronicle.com/nation/current-affairs/270717/karnataka-snakes-are-farmers-friends-say-experts.html ;
  Indore forest department urging people not to offer milk to snakes (Free Press Journal):
  https://www.freepressjournal.in/indore/indore-35-snakes-rescued-on-nag-panchami-forest-dept-urges-people-not-to-give-milk-to-snakes ;
  Hans India, Hyderabad activists: https://www.thehansindia.com/telangana/hyderabad-dont-feed-milk-to-snakes-plead-activists-635040
- **Snakes are protected wildlife in India.** Catching or hurting them is an offence under the Wild Life (Protection) Act, 1972. Sources:
  experts quoted in Deccan Herald https://www.deccanherald.com/amp/story/india%2Fsave-snakes-atrocities-nag-panchami-2433911
  and Free Press Journal https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/maharashtra-news-wildlife-experts-slam-state-govts-move-to-revive-cobra-worship-on-nag-panchami-call-practice-illegal-and-cruel .
  **UNVERIFIED:** which Schedule the Indian cobra is in, and the exact jail and fine figures. The sources disagree, and I couldn't check the
  2022 amendment schedules, so don't put them on screen.
- **Suggested gentle wording** (offered as a choice, never as a correction): "ఒక చిన్న విషయం: పాములు నిజానికి పాలు తాగవు, అవి ఎలుకలు, కప్పలు తింటాయి.

  comes from one source only: https://hindutone.com/festivals/do-snakes-drink-milk-nagula-chavithi/ . Hans India has also
  reported that pouring large amounts of milk into pits can harm snakes: https://thehansindia.com/news/cities/visakhapatnam/rituals-that-frighten-hissing-gods-714267
