## Premise options (the "would I send this to a friend?" test)
1. **(chosen) The missed call.** Everyone expects the refused startup offer; he picks the evening he cut his father's
   call for a sprint deadline. Send-to-friend test: yes — every 25–34 professional has a "తర్వాత చేస్తాలే" call, and
   the reel literally ends with "send this to your dad". The twist flips the viewer's own assumption (money → people).
2. The resignation e-mail. He redoes the day he *didn't* quit a toxic job — and in the replay he quits, then sees the
   other path wasn't better. Clever, but a career-advice reel the audience has seen many times; weaker emotion.
3. The bus ticket. He redoes the Sankranti he skipped home to save leave — replays it, and the only change is the
   family photo now has him in it. Warm and very local, but close to option 1 with a softer twist; keep as a backup
   for Sankranti (Jan 2027).

is natural. **Why 2022, not 2021:** a 2021 date + father would read as a COVID-wave death to many Telugu viewers —
too heavy and too specific; 2022 keeps it gentle. No illness, hospital or death is shown or mentioned.

## Retention pass
- **Drop risk 1 — L4–L5 (two "expected" days):** could feel like a list. Fix: the calendar flips fast with a visual per
  card (rupee pile, MISSED flight stamp), each line ≤ 10 words, and a 6-frame "Tue · 9:40 PM 📞" page flashes past
  as a rewatch clue (a real plant, not a random interrupt). If the reel runs over 45 s, **cut L5 first** (keep the
  wedding card on screen without a line).
- **Drop risk 2 — L6 follow aside:** one brisk line while the calendar freezes mid-flip, then straight into the question.
- **Drop risk 3 — the 5 s countdown:** day-cards fan out as choices + comment bubbles float up (people see other
  people's days — it pulls them in) and the clock ticks.
- **Drop risk 4 — after the reveal:** the father's line is the emotional peak; only 2 short lines follow and the last
  one is a loop (the notification pings again), so the reel restarts instead of ending.
- Cut: no explanation of the "app" or the magic, no moral line, no "life is short" sermon.
