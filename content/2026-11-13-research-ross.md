## 2. The discovery: what, when, where

| Fact | Source(s) |
|---|---|
| On **20 August 1897**, in **Secunderabad**, Ross dissected the stomach tissue of an anopheline mosquito that had fed **four days earlier** on a malaria patient and found the malaria parasite | **KEY** CDC (US Centers for Disease Control), "Ross and the Discovery that Mosquitoes Transmit Malaria Parasites": https://www.cdc.gov/malaria/about/history/ross.html (archived copy: https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/eot2008/20090507195436/http:/www.cdc.gov/malaria/history/ross.htm) |

**What he called the mosquito:** Ross didn't know the species. He called them "dappled-winged" (also "brown") mosquitoes,
and they were later identified as *Anopheles*: EBSCO (above); Hektoen International: https://hekint.org/2017/02/01/ronald-ross-polymath-and-discoverer/ .
Safe script wording: "మచ్చల రెక్కల దోమ… తర్వాత దానికి Anopheles అని పేరు" or simply "Anopheles దోమ".

**The patient:** a malaria patient named **Husein (Hussain) Khan** let the mosquitoes feed on him for **8 annas** (one anna per
fed mosquito). Sources: EBSCO (above), Hektoen (above), Wikipedia (above). Secondary sources only, and the spelling varies.
If used, say "ఒక malaria patient, 8 అణాలకి". Don't put a name on screen unless the owner is happy with the secondary sourcing.

### World Mosquito Day = 20 August, because of this
- Ross himself said 20 August should be remembered each year. LSTM (Liverpool School of Tropical Medicine): https://www.lstmed.ac.uk/node/719 ;
  ISID: https://isid.org/world-mosquito-day/ ; RSTMH: https://rstmh.org/node/759 ; Osmania SRRIP page (above): "remembered as World Mosquito Day".
- In his *Memoirs* (1923, p. 223) he calls it "Mosquito Day". The printed text says 1895 by mistake; it was 1897 (Wikipedia, citing the Memoirs): https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronald_Ross
- LSHTM has marked it since the 1930s (a 1931 Ross Institute tea party is the usual origin story). Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Mosquito_Day . **UNVERIFIED** against an LSHTM page.

**Checking "first born in India":** the only earlier prizes are 1901 (Röntgen, van 't Hoff, von Behring, Sully Prudhomme,
Dunant, Passy) and 1902's other laureates (Lorentz, Zeeman, Fischer, Mommsen, Ducommun, Gobat). All were born in Europe
(my own check from general knowledge; nobelprize.org couldn't be opened to re-confirm). So Ross, born in Almora, is the first
India-born laureate. This agrees with "first born outside Europe" (Wikipedia). **Status: well supported but only by
secondary sources.** Wording must not suggest he was Indian. He was British, born in India. Tagore (1913) is the first
Indian and first Asian laureate. A nice surprising beat: "India లో పుట్టిన మొదటి Nobel winner… Tagore కంటే 11 years ముందు."
Arithmetic: 1913 − 1902 = 11 ✓.

## 4. Surprising, lesser-known details (sourced)

1. **He wanted to be a writer, not a doctor.** At 17 he wanted to write (some say be an artist), but his father pushed him into
   medicine. He studied at St Bartholomew's, London, and joined the **Indian Medical Service in 1881**. Wikipedia (above);
   malariasite.com: https://www.malariasite.com/ronald-ross/ ; Encyclopedia.com: https://www.encyclopedia.com/people/medicine/medicine-biographies/ronald-ross .
   He kept writing all his life: novels (*The Child of Ocean*, *The Spirit of Storm*), plays, poems, music and maths books.
   RCPSG heritage blog: https://heritageblog.rcpsg.ac.uk/2014/04/25/sir-ronald-ross-a-man-of-many-talents/ ; EBSCO: https://ebsco.com/research-starters/history/ronald-ross
2. **He wrote a poem the night of the discovery.** It begins "This day relenting God / Hath placed within my hand / A wondrous thing…"
   and ends "O million-murdering Death" (about malaria). Encyclopedia.com (reprinting his 1923 *Memoirs*): https://encyclopedia.com/science/medical-magazines/day-relenting-god ;
   Christian History Institute: https://christianhistoryinstitute.org/dailyquote/8/20 ; LSHTM library blog: https://blogs.lshtm.ac.uk/library/?p=5847 .
   The poem is in *Philosophies* (1910, public domain, Project Gutenberg #54870): https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/54870 , dated 21 Aug 1897 there.
   **Caveat:** wording differs slightly between copies ("relenting/designing", "placed/put"), and so does the date (night of the 20th vs the 21st).
   Rule-safe use: quote **one short line only**, attributed ("— Ronald Ross, 1897"). "O million-murdering Death" is the strongest. Better still, paraphrase it in Telugu.
3. **The Calcutta bird experiments completed the proof (1898).** Moved to Calcutta with no human malaria cases to hand, he
   followed Manson's suggestion and studied bird malaria in sparrows with *Culex* mosquitoes. He found the parasites gather in the
   mosquito's **salivary glands** (4 July 1898) and showed that infected mosquitoes give healthy birds malaria. Garrison-Morton: https://www.historyofmedicine.com/id/7904 (search snippet);
   Wikipedia (above); EBSCO (above); The Telegraph: https://obeta.ttef.in/my-kolkata/people/how-sir-ronald-ross-established-the-complete-life-cycle-of-malaria-parasite-and-the-significance-of-august-20/cid/2042285
4. **Right after the breakthrough he was posted away.** In Sept 1897 he was sent to Bombay and then to **Kherwara, Rajputana**,
   which had no malaria, so the human work stopped. Wikipedia (above); PMC article on his memoirs: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5530821
5. **A Nobel winner who sold his own papers.** In **1928** Ross sold his malaria papers. **Lady Houston bought them for £2,000** and gave
   them to the Ross Institute (now in the LSHTM archives). AIM25 archive record: https://aim25.com/cats/37/4046.htm ; Archives Hub: https://archiveshub.ac.uk/search/archives/9fbbdb5b-f639-3bba-80e3-3f1f4c6e3aa6 ;
   LSHTM, Martin 2022, "Who was Sir Ronald Ross" (cites LSHTM Ross/146/30): https://lshtm-eprints-repo-prod.lshtm-eprints.cdl.cosector.com/4664790/3/Martin_2022_Who-was-sir-ronald-ross.pdf ;
   RCS Plarr's Lives (above): "at no time was Ross a rich man". One 1928 NZ newspaper says £3,000. The archive figure, **£2,000**, is the one to use.

## 6. Arithmetic (all checked)

- Age at discovery: 1897 − 1857 = **40**. The birthday (13 May) was before 20 Aug, so he was 40 ✓.
- Age at the Nobel (Dec 1902): 1902 − 1857 = **45** ✓.
- Discovery to Nobel: 1902 − 1897 = **5 years** ✓.
- Mosquitoes fed 16 Aug, dissected 20 Aug: 20 − 16 = **4 days** ✓ (matches the CDC's "four days previously").
- Plaque: 1935 − 1897 = **38 years** after ✓. Museum and Fever Hospital renaming in 1997: 1997 − 1897 = **100**, the centenary ✓.
- Papers sold 1928: 1928 − 1902 = **26 years** after the Nobel ✓.
- Died 16 Sep 1932: 1932 − 1857 = **75** (birthday passed) ✓.
- Since the discovery: 2026 − 1897 = **129 years** ✓.
- Ross vs Tagore: 1913 − 1902 = **11 years** ✓.
- "After three years of failure" (Christian History Institute wording): Ross arrived in Bombay 21 Apr 1895 (Osmania page). Apr 1895 to Aug 1897
  is about **2 years 4 months**, but his mosquito hunt began after meeting Manson in late 1894. So "~2–3 years" is fair.
  **Don't say "three years" as a hard number.** Say "దాదాపు రెండేళ్లకు పైగా ప్రయత్నం".
