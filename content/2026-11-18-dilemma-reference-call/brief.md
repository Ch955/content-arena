## Changes from the manager's premise
- **Kiran → Sandeep.** The 15 Nov reel ("Sunday client call") already has a Kiran: the *new junior (~23)* who fixes the
  bug from Nikhil's runbook. A Kiran who took Nikhil's credit *last year* would contradict that, so the slick colleague
  is now Sandeep (34). Revert only if the owner doesn't mind the clash.
- **Why the HR calls Nikhil (plot hole closed):** the hiring company's form asks for a peer from the candidate's biggest
  project. Sandeep's biggest claimed project is the Q3 release, which Nikhil led, so he had to list him (on the form chip
  in L1). That's also why Sandeep watches Nikhil leave the stand-up uneasily in frame 1.
- **How Sandeep "heard" what Nikhil said:** HR doesn't tell him (real HR teams don't disclose references). He *works it
  out*: in his 5 PM final round HR asked him specifically about Q3, and only the Q3 reference would prompt that. His line
  "HR Q3 గురించే అడిగారు…" carries it. This survives a rewatch better than "HR told me".
- **Nikhil's answer is sharper than "ask him":** he names his own bias ("Credit విషయంలో నేను biased…"). It isn't a dodge or
  a lie: he says the one good thing he knows first-hand, declares his conflict of interest and hands the question to
  the only person who should answer it. That is the "C" the viewer didn't see coming, and it gives comments a third
  side to argue about.
- **The turn is quiet:** no hug, no offer email, no speech. Sandeep says three short things; Nikhil slides him a chai.
  Whether Sandeep gets the offer stays off-screen on purpose (it isn't the reel's question; the dilemma is answered).
- The afternoon "stand-up" is the 3:30 PM US-overlap stand-up (common in Hitech City teams), so it isn't odd that it's
  in the afternoon.

## Premise options (the "would I send this to a friend?" test)
1. **(chosen) The reference call.** Every 25–34 office-goer has had (or been) the colleague who took the credit, and
   reference checks are a real, slightly scary part of switching jobs. The stakes are concrete in the first 3 seconds
   (a named person, ₹30 LPA, "your word decides it"), the A/B is genuinely hard (karma vs. a mother on dialysis, and
   lying to a stranger who trusts you), and the ending is a third option most viewers won't think of. Send-to-friend
   test: **yes**. "Tag the colleague who took your credit 😤" is a joke people can play on an office friend, and the
   A/B/C question is easy to answer with one letter.
2. **The salary slip on the printer.** Nikhil finds his junior's payslip on the office printer: she earns ₹4 L more than
   him for the same role. Tell the manager, use it in his appraisal, or shred it? Strong and relatable, but it's about
   money envy, the ending is likely preachy, and it leans on a "how did it get there" setup that eats seconds.
3. **The farewell-gift money.** Nikhil collects for a team farewell gift; one colleague never paid his share and has
   just been laid off. Cover it quietly, or ask him in front of the group? Kind and real, but the stakes are small and
   the "right" answer is obvious, so it fails the hard-A/B test.

## Angle
- Belief before: "If someone wronged you and fate hands you the knife, either you use it (truth = revenge) or you
  rise above it (cover = kindness). Those are the only two options."
  After: "There is a third one: say only what is fair, admit your own bias and let the person answer for himself.
  It's not revenge and not a lie, and it gave him the chance to fix it."
  Why they stop: line 1 is a stranger telling you a ₹30 LPA offer depends on *your* word; line 2 tells you whose offer it

- Alt angle 2: "Should references be honest at all?" (a workplace explainer; too dry).
- Alt angle 3: "Karma is real: the credit-stealer gets caught" (revenge porn for the comments; cheap and preachy).

## Plot-hole check (the twist must survive a rewatch)
- **Why is Nikhil the reference?** The form asks for a peer from the candidate's biggest project (Q3, led by Nikhil). On
  the L1 form chip: "Peer reference (Q3 release): NIKHIL R".
- **Why is Sandeep uneasy in frame 1?** He knows the call is coming today (final round at 5 PM, references before it).
- **Does Nikhil lie?** No. "Pressure లో deliver చేస్తాడు" is true (Sandeep is good under pressure; he isn't a fraud,
  he grabbed one credit). "నేను biased" is true. He doesn't conceal the credit issue either: he points HR straight at
  it. Viewers can still argue that it's a soft way to blow the whistle, and that argument is the comment section.
- **Does Nikhil sink him?** Not by himself: HR asks Sandeep, and Sandeep chooses to tell the truth. Nikhil's fairness
  gave him the chance.
- **How does Sandeep know?** He works it out (HR asked about Q3 specifically; only the Q3 reference explains that). HR
  never discloses a reference, which keeps it realistic.
- **Timeline:** stand-up 3:30 → call ~3:40–3:44 → final round 5:00 → chai 6:40, same day. Clocks on screen match.
- **Money/details consistent everywhere:** ₹30 LPA, "Senior Engineer", Q3 release, "last year", dialysis "this month",
  final round 5 PM. Caption and YouTube text use the same.
- **Continuity with earlier reels:** 14 Nov (Nikhil missed a promotion demo *this year*) and this reel (missed a
  promotion *last year*) don't contradict; if anything they make him consistent: he keeps putting people first. Suresh (TL,
  15 Nov) isn't needed here. Kiran renamed (see above).
- Rewatch clues: Sandeep's uneasy look in frame 1, the "Q3 RELEASE STAR · SANDEEP" certificate on his desk, the form chip
  ("Peer reference (Q3 release)", "Final round TODAY 5:00 PM"), and the deploy log "deployed by nikhil.r" in the flashback.
